This manuscript fits Frontiers in Genetics, specialty Statistical Genetics and Methodology. We present a six-gate falsification framework that corrects locus-driven confounding in two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR), a core statistical-genetic tool for causal inference from genome-wide association data. The work is methodological: it defines explicit failure modes (association, robustness to LD/overlap/pleiotropy, genetic architecture, direct effect, biological mediation, external validation), derives a fixed a-priori evidence grade, and benchmarks operating characteristics across 43,200 simulated MR scenarios. An empirical proof-of-concept reclassifies the reported insomnia-to-Alzheimer's disease signal as shared APOE-locus liability (Grade C), showing the framework's utility for genetic-epidemiology causal claims. Full reproducibility (R 4.5.2 renv lockfile, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21858125, GitHub ming13333/iad-falsification-framework) meets the journal's standards for transparent, reusable methods. The contribution is a general statistical-genetic methodology, not a trait-specific discovery, so it belongs in Statistical Genetics and Methodology rather than a disease- or trait-specific specialty.
