This manuscript fits Frontiers in Genetics, specialty Statistical Genetics and Methodology. We present a six-gate falsification framework that diagnoses locus-driven confounding in two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR), a core statistical-genetic tool for causal inference from genome-wide association data. The work is methodological: it defines six explicit failure modes (association, robustness to LD, sample overlap and pleiotropy, genetic architecture, direct effect, biological mediation, external validation), derives a fixed a-priori evidence grade (A-D, 0-12 points), and reports a 43,200-replicate benchmark of the scoring rule under proxy gate implementations, which characterises the score-to-grade mapping rather than the operating characteristics of the genomic estimators. An empirical application reclassifies the reported insomnia-to-Alzheimer's disease signal as shared APOE-locus liability (Grade C). The pipeline is reproducible (R 4.5.2 with a renv.lock for the genomic analyses, Python 3.13.14 with a requirements.txt for the simulation) and archived at Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23733172) and GitHub (ming13333/iad-falsification-framework). The contribution is a general statistical-genetic methodology, not a trait-specific discovery, so it belongs in Statistical Genetics and Methodology rather than a disease- or trait-specific specialty.
